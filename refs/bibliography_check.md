## Key numbers quoted verbatim from abstracts

- **Suarez 1998:** "hydrogen sulphide (1.06 (0.2) mumol/l), followed by methanethiol (0.21 (0.04) mumol/l) and dimethyl sulphide (0.08 (0.01) mumol/l) (means (SEM))"; "Malodour significantly correlated with hydrogen sulphide concentration"; "The cushion absorbed more than 90% of the sulphur gases." ← relevant to the cushion-reservoir scenario.
- **Suarez 1997:** "Gases produced intraluminally (H2, CO2, and CH4) comprised approximately 74% of flatus"; "The predominant flatus gas was CO2, H2, and N2 in seven, six, and three subjects, respectively."
- **Tomlin 1991:** "Total daily volume ranged from 476 to 1491 ml (median 705 ml)." Fibre-free diet: "median 214 ml/24 h".
- **Furne & Levitt 1996:** "subjects passed gas 10 +/- 1 times/day [upper limit of normal (mean + 2 SD): 20 times/day]".
- **Manichanh 2014:** "7.4±1.0 daytime evacuations"; gas evacuated after a standard meal "262±22 and 265±25 mL".
- **Bolin & Stanton 1998:** "Daily flatus emissions averaged 12.7 (range to 2-53) for men and 7.1 (range 1-32) for women."
- No abstract reports a **volume per passage**; it is derived as daily volume / passages (Tomlin median 705 mL ÷ Furne–Levitt 10 /day ≈ 70 mL; range ≈ 476/20 ≈ 24 mL to 1491/10 ≈ 150 mL). Status: derived, not measured.
- **Licina 2014:** "reduction of the CBL mean peak velocity from 0.24 to 0.16 m/s in front of the sitting manikin" (20 → 26 °C); clothing "reduces the peak velocity in the breathing zone from 0.205 m/s by 17%, and by 40% for thick-loose ensemble"; table gap closed: "0.17 to 0.111 m/s".
- **Licina 2015:** downward flow at 0.30 m/s "collides with the CBL at the nose level reducing the peak velocity from 0.185 to 0.10 m/s".
- **McGinley 2004:** laboratory olfactometry H₂S thresholds "0.64-1.3 microg/m3 (0.45-0.9 ppbv)"; field olfactometers 0.5–2.0 ppbv.
- **Nagata 2003, Table 2 (ppm v/v):** H₂S 0.00041; methyl mercaptan 0.000070; dimethyl sulfide 0.0030.
- **Greenman 2004:** "comparison of slopes showed H(2)S to be the most significant in terms of odor power."
- **Collins 2001 (dogs):** odour rating = 1.51 × [H₂S]^0.28 — a Stevens-type exponent of 0.28 for flatus odour (dogs' flatus, human judge) ← useful for the perception module.
- **Ancalle 2021:** sound is produced by "the vibration of the skin of the anus, similar to the buzzing of lips in a trumpet's mouthpiece" (supports the lip-reed model; no frequency given in the abstract).
- **Greenberg 2013:** recent exposure can alter "subsequent detection thresholds by up to three orders of magnitude" (adaptation).
- **Dowling & Dimotakis 1990:** abstract gives no decay constant (Re = 5000, 16000, 40000; x/d = 20–90). Constant must come from full text.

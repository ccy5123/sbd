\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Every parameter, with its source, range and whether it is measured, derived or assumed, is listed in
Table~\ref{SI-tab:params}. Code and data are openly archived (Data accessibility).

\subsection{Source}
\label{sec:source}
Passage volumes span 17--375\,mL, with a median of 92\,mL \cite{suarez1997}. In the Monte Carlo analysis, volume $V$,
duration $T$ (0.3--20\,s) and effective orifice diameter $d$ (1--8\,mm) are drawn log-uniformly. Neither duration nor
diameter has been measured for a passage; their upper bounds come from anorectal physiology
(Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-acoustics}) and the lower bounds are assumed.

Sulfur gas concentrations are taken from 87 passages of 16 volunteers \cite{suarez1998}: \ce{H2S}
1.06\,\textmu mol\,L$^{-1}$, methanethiol 0.21\,\textmu mol\,L$^{-1}$ and dimethyl sulfide 0.08\,\textmu mol\,L$^{-1}$.
They vary between passages by a log-normal factor with $\sigma_{\ln} = 1.1$, derived from the reported standard
errors, drawn independently of volume because sulfur concentration does not correlate with passage volume
\cite{suarez1997}. Buoyancy, computed from measured compositions, is likewise drawn independently of volume
(Spearman $-0.15$ across the 16 subjects).

\subsection{Odorants and sound}
\label{sec:methods-chem}
\label{sec:acoustics}
Details are in Sections~\ref{SI-sec:si-chem} and \ref{SI-sec:si-acoustics}. Each sulfur gas contributes an odour
activity value, its mole fraction over its detection threshold; the passage's OAV is their sum (no mixture
suppression). We compare two measured compositions \cite{suarez1998,tangerman2009} under three threshold sets, from a
trained panel \cite{nagata2003} to population means \cite{amoore1983}. Gas-phase losses use IUPAC rate
constants \cite{atkinson2004} and office oxidant levels \cite{fiorentino2023}.

The anal canal is modelled as a lip reed, a soft-tissue valve that self-oscillates in the flow through it, as the
lips do on a brass instrument \cite{ancalle2021}. It oscillates when the driving pressure
$\tfrac12\rho(u_0/C_\mathrm{d})^2$ exceeds a threshold of 0.6--2.5\,kPa, bracketed by brass playing
\cite{matteoli2021} and by oesophageal speech, the closest measured soft-tissue sphincter source \cite{nagai1993}.
This gives an onset exit velocity of about 42\,m\,s$^{-1}$ (21--71). An oscillating valve radiates as a harmonic
monopole with a fundamental of 160\,Hz (60--300\,Hz). A release counts as \emph{audible} when a harmonic exceeds both
the hearing threshold \cite{iso226_2023} and the masked threshold of the background, from a 35\,dB(A) living room to
a 77\,dB(A) subway car.

\subsection{Analytic dispersion model}
\label{sec:gate0}
Dispersion is modelled in two phases. In the near field the release behaves as a starting jet and then a puff, or as
a buoyant thermal, until its self-induced velocity has fallen to the ambient velocity $u_h$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app-nearfield}). The cloud is then advected to the nose and spreads as a Gaussian puff,
$\sigma^2 = \sigma_0^2 + 2Kt$, with indoor eddy diffusivity $K = 10^{-4}$--$1.3\times10^{-2}$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$
\cite{cheng2011}. Two receptors are modelled: the releaser's own nose, reached by the convective boundary layer, the
layer of warm air rising along the body \cite{licina2014}, at a speed taken from the CFD trajectories below; and a
neighbour 0.8--1.4\,m away, reached by room air. Concentrations are averaged over one inspiration.

We draw 20\,000 pairs of silent and audible releases, each classified by the acoustic onset and evaluated with
environmental parameters drawn from their ranges. Three simplifications favour the silent release: turbulent-jet
constants are used even for laminar silent releases, the nose sits on the cloud centreline, and the centreline
concentration is used.

\subsection{Computational fluid dynamics}
\label{sec:cfd}
We use OpenFOAM 13 with transient Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes equations (RANS), the $k$--$\omega$ SST turbulence
model, the Boussinesq approximation for buoyancy and the energy equation in internal-energy form.

\emph{Scenes} (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-scenes}). Manikins built from geometric primitives, with skin at
33\,\textdegree C, stand or sit in rooms at 23\,\textdegree C:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a sofa: two seated people 0.6\,m apart in a 3.2 $\times$ 3.2 $\times$ 2.6\,m living room, with the releaser
  upright, reclined by 4.5\,\textdegree\ or leaning forward by 10\,\textdegree;
  \item an elevator: four standing people in a closed 1.4 $\times$ 1.1 $\times$ 2.1\,m car, fan off, with the releaser
  in the front row and a person behind;
  \item a standing pair: two people face to face, noses 1.0\,m apart, in the same living room, in still air and with
  office ceiling ventilation at 2.9 air changes per hour (EN 16798-1 Category II \cite{olesen2024}).
\end{itemize}
The upright sofa and both standing rooms are also run with long trousers on everyone: a porous shell over pelvis and
thighs, 2\,cm from the skin, with the air permeability of denim and open at the waist and hems.

\emph{Releases.} Each room first runs 90\,s without a release, until the body plumes have developed. Four
representative passages are then released behind the releaser's buttocks, 30\,s apart, each carried by its own
passive scalar so that their doses can be read separately: the median and 90th percentile of the exit momentum flux
$M_0 = Q_0u_0$ within each class of the source model (silent: 122\,mL over 4.8\,s at $u_0 = 3.5$\,m\,s$^{-1}$, and
66\,mL at 22\,m\,s$^{-1}$; audible: 86\,mL at 94\,m\,s$^{-1}$, and 279\,mL at 162\,m\,s$^{-1}$). Each enters as an
inlet that carries the release's momentum and odorant flux, with its buoyancy as an equivalent temperature excess,
and is followed for 150\,s. Later exposure is taken from the well-mixed room level (Theorem~2), which predicts 0.67--1.00 times
the 600-s worst breath of two coupled 10-minute runs. The odorant is sampled 2.5\,cm in front
of each nose over sinusoidal breathing cycles.

\emph{Peak factor.} RANS gives the ensemble-mean concentration, while a nose meets filaments. Large-eddy simulations
(WALE subgrid model) of the upright sofa, bare and in trousers, repeat the releases six times (the medians twice) and
give the ratio of the large-eddy to the RANS worst breath for each release and nose.

\emph{Validation} (Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-validation}). A free round jet reproduces the canonical decay constants
within a grid uncertainty of 3.7\%. The
convective boundary layer of a seated manikin is 40--56\% faster than thermal-manikin data \cite{licina2014} and
follows their trend with room temperature. For a source at a seated manikin's groin \cite{licina2017}, the scene
settings give a breathing-zone intake fraction of 8.1, 9.1 and 11.0 per mille over 10, 22 and 100\,min, against
5.5, 7.7 and 9.8 measured. Licina et al.\ also report the manikin switched off; without body heat the flow near the
source is set by the supply diffusers, the lighting load and the temperature of the aerosol stream, none of which is
reported, and every scene here contains heated occupants, so we compare the heated case only. Refining the sofa mesh
by 1.5 in every direction (0.72 to 2.2 million cells) changes the median silent dose by a factor 0.55 at the
neighbour and 3.0 at the releaser's own nose.

\subsection{Perception and observers}
\label{sec:perception}
The nose samples only during inhalation: sinusoidal flow at 12 breaths per minute, inspiratory fraction 0.42
\cite{wysocki2006}. Perceived intensity follows Stevens' power law, $I \propto \mathrm{OAV}^n$, with $n = 0.28$
measured for flatus \ce{H2S} \cite{collins2001}. Two passages are distinguishable when their concentrations differ by
more than the olfactory Weber fraction of 0.28 \cite{stone1965}, i.e.\ a just-noticeable ratio of 1.28.

The OAVs of the dispersion model use trained-panel thresholds \cite{nagata2003}. For untrained observers:
\begin{itemize}
  \item population thresholds are 10--23 times higher \cite{amoore1983,wilby1969};
  \item detection is a per-passage probability $\Phi(\log_2\mathrm{OAV}/\sigma)$ with $\sigma = 2$ binary steps
  \cite{amoore1983}, since one person's threshold varies over time about as much as thresholds vary across people
  \cite{stevens1988};
  \item a bystander who is not expecting an odour needs 26 times the threshold for a 50\% response, with
  $\sigma = 3.5$ binary steps \cite{amoore1983,whisman1978}.
\end{itemize}
Distraction reduces noticing \cite{forster2018} but not the peak rated intensity \cite{fallon2018}, so intensity is
computed from the attentive OAV. Two variants follow: clothing removes 22\% of the sulfide gas \cite{ohge2005}, and a
sound can make a distracted bystander attentive for that passage.

\subsection{Uncertainty analysis}
\label{sec:uq}
Input uncertainty is propagated through the analytic model; one multi-release CFD run costs one to several days on
four to eighteen cores, too many for a sampling design. First-order Sobol indices (the share of the variance of $\log_{10} R$ explained by one input
alone) are estimated by binning the Monte Carlo sample, with bootstrap 90\% intervals. Because the silent and audible
releases of a pair are drawn by rejection, the inputs are dependent and the indices are descriptive. The regime map
shows $\log_{10} R$ at equal mass against the audible release's Silencer number and the travel time.

\emph{Passages as emitted in the simulated scenes.} The 20\,000 passages of the source model each have a volume
$V$, an exit momentum flux $M_0$ and a class. At each nose a passage gets the dose per unit volume of the
representative releases of its class, interpolated in $\log M_0$ and held constant outside their range, times $V$:
the odorant is a passive scalar, so the dose is linear in volume, and the flow path is assumed to depend on the
momentum but not on the volume. Its hearing at each nose comes from the acoustic model. Pairs of one silent and one
audible passage then fall into four outcomes: neither smelled; silent worse (only the silent one smelled, or both and
the silent one above the just-noticeable ratio); audible worse (the reverse); and a tie. A bystander remembers a
silent passage only if smelled (Theorem~3), so the remembered shares are the same outcomes among pairs whose silent
passage was smelled. Scenes are weighted by the time an adult spends close to another person in each setting
(sofa 0.73, elevator 0.005, standing 0.26; Section~\ref{SI-sec:si-weights}), with home- and office-centred days as
bounds. In the clothed variants each passage is in trousers with probability 0.7 or 0.85, independently.
